Part I · Micro-Scale · Control Foundation05 / 28

Fractional-Order Visual Servoing Control of Quadrotor UAS

Why Visual Servoing Breaks Near Structures

  • Under-bridge flight denies GPS and introduces aerodynamic turbulence & wind gusts.
  • Onboard vision processing creates larger, variable sampling periods that destabilize integer-order PID loops.
  • Target loss from camera FOV causes immediate mission failure.

Iso-Damping Fractional-Order Design (FOPD)

  • Extends derivative/integral action to real orders (λ, μ), enforcing zero phase slope at gain crossover: d arg G(jω)/dω = 0.
  • Built reproducible Simscape & OS4 benchmarks plus custom Pixhawk + embedded Linux + ground HSV tuning stack.
  • Extended to RGB-D cave SLAM in SmartCaveDrone (ICUAS '17).
Experimental Quadrotor Visual Servoing Architecture (Pixhawk + Odroid Linux + Ground HSV Calibration)
Shang et al., Nonlinear Dynamics (2019); ICUAS (2016); ICARCV (2018); Zhang, Shang et al., ICUAS (2017) Bo Shang, Ph.D. · UMD M-TRAIL · bshang@umd.edu
Part I · Micro-Scale · Experimental Validation06 / 28

Sampling-Period Tolerance & Gain-Invariance Under Vision Latency

0.933 s vs. 0.546 s (+71%)

Maximum stable sampling period achieved by FOPD vs. integer-order PID (ASME IDETC 2017), tolerating lower-rate onboard vision.

20% Flight Error Reduction

Outdoor and indoor precision hovering experiments showed 20% lower tracking error and flatter ITAE growth across sampling periods.

Design Principle Carried Forward

Sampling period, sensor geometry, and actuation margin are explicit design inputs—not noise to be averaged away downstream.

Step Response Across Sampling Periods h = 0.058 s to 0.58 s: IOPID Oscillates at 0.58 s While FOPD Stays Damped

BIRDS: Hybrid Flying & Surface-Traversing Bridge Inspection Robot

Operational Motivation & System Concept

  • 42% of U.S. bridges are 50+ years old; snooper trucks block lanes and free-flying UAVs drain batteries fighting under-deck wind.
  • BIRDS flies into position, clamps onto an I-girder bottom flange, shuts down rotors, and wheels along the beam for close-range sensing.

Part II · Meso-Scale · Geometric Observability13 / 28

PACO: Parametric Assessment of Cooperative Observability

5-Stage Parametric Evaluation Pipeline

  1. Ego Instantiation: Place virtual onboard sensors on real vehicle trajectories.
  2. Sensor Coverage: Compute parametric horizontal FOV & range sectors.
  3. Occlusion Resolution: Ray-tracing-free angular-wedge shadow model.
  4. Cooperative Augmentation: Fuse ego + elevated roadside V2I LOS.
  5. Multi-Scope Metrics: Scene-wide vs. active conflict-partner recall.

Empirical Trajectory Foundation

  • Driven by 68 minutes (40,793 frames at 10 Hz) of dense multi-class NYC intersection trajectories.
  • Evaluates exact line-of-sight geometry rather than synthetic empty-road assumptions.
  • Accepted at TRB Annual Meeting; in preparation for IEEE T-ITS.
Angular-Wedge Shadow Model: Elevated Roadside LiDAR Sees Into Truck Occlusion Cone
Shang & Li, SSRN 6867604 (2026) · Accepted at TRB Annual Meeting Bo Shang, Ph.D. · UMD M-TRAIL · bshang@umd.edu
Part II · Meso-Scale · Key Quantitative Finding14 / 28

The Observability Gap: Occlusion—Not FOV—Is the Binding Constraint

Mathematical Necessity of Infrastructure Sensing: Expanding vehicle FOV from 120° ADAS (0.37) to 360° AV (0.61) still leaves 39% of the intersection scene unobservable—and occluded objects double from 1.7 to 3.3 per frame. Only elevated V2I LiDAR closes the gap to 1.00.

Part III · Macro-Scale · Scalable Freight Intelligence19 / 28

Drive-By Urban Freight Sensing & Training-Free VLM Classification

Drive-By Urban Freight Sensing (IEEE OJ-ITS 2026)

  • Fixed sensors cover key corridors, but freight planners also need city-wide curb and arterial truck distributions without installing hardware on every block.
  • Developed a deep-learning pipeline for large-scale, fine-grained truck body classification from Google Street View imagery.
  • Turns opportunistic street-level imagery into scalable freight exposure maps across urban neighborhoods (open access, Sept 2026).

Bridging the LiDAR Modality Gap with VLMs

  • Re-annotating 3D bounding boxes and FHWA truck classes for every new roadside LiDAR geometry is a major deployment bottleneck.
  • Co-developed training-free and few-shot Vision-Language Model (VLM) frameworks (arXiv:2602.09425; TRB 2026) that project 3D roadside point clouds into structured visual views.
  • Enables zero/few-shot vehicle & truck classification across new sensor configurations.

Macro-Scale Synthesis

Combines high-frequency 24/7 corridor depth on highways (InfraLiDAR-Eval) with city-wide spatial breadth (Street View freight sensing) and low-annotation cross-site transfer (VLM point-cloud classification).

Part IV · City & Network Scale · Simulation & Governance22 / 28

LLM-SUMO Traffic Digital Twin & Auditable AI Standards

LLM-SUMO Integration on CERRA (Live Oct 2026)

  • CERRA itself is the geospatial platform; inside it, LLM-SUMO serves as a working traffic-simulation digital twin.
  • Authored by Jihoon Kim; ported to Linux, connected via a protected execution bridge, packaged as a CERRA module, and operated by Bo Shang.
  • Converts natural-language prompts into calibrated SUMO network scenarios, executes runs, and summarizes traffic impacts for engineers.

Auditable Decision Support & PAR Standard

  • Joins empirical before/after corridor records (e.g., 25,024 directional XD speed segments & HIN crash data) with SUMO counterfactuals.
  • Every copilot response must return four elements: Observed Data + Simulation Result + Explicit Assumptions + Sources.
  • Observing real failure modes motivated leading a PAR-style Recommended Practice effort on how agencies audit AI-generated traffic studies.

Engineering Boundary

AI-driven simulation explores counterfactuals—it does not replace the traffic engineer. A decision or study an operator cannot audit is a decision a public agency will not deploy.

Part IV · City & Network Scale · Constrained Optimization & Security23 / 28

Feasibility-Preserving Routing on Quantum Hardware & Quantum-Safe V2X

Feasibility-Preserving VRP on IBM Heron (Oct 2026)

  • Penalty-based QUBO encodings often return infeasible routes when solver noise increases.
  • Formulated vehicle-routing problems in feasibility-preserving encodings and benchmarked them across classical GA/SA solvers, quantum simulators, and an IBM Heron superconducting processor.
  • Honest Empirical Finding: At tested hardware scale, quantum execution shows no advantage over classical baselines; the enduring contribution is the structural feasibility guarantee.

Quantum-Safe V2X Signal Broadcast & Agent Eval

  • Evaluates post-quantum cryptographic authentication overhead on roadside SPaT (Signal Phase & Timing) broadcasts so cooperative safety stays trustworthy under strict V2I latency budgets.
  • Applies reproducible evaluation (versioned configs, counterbalanced replay, regression guardrails) to tool-using AI agents—documented in open research notes.

Generalizable Lesson Across Physical & Algorithmic Systems

Problem structure—occlusion geometry at intersections, feasibility encodings in routing, and source provenance in AI—rather than raw compute or sensor count is usually the binding constraint in field deployment.
